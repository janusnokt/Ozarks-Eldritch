The Two Crazed Crows, to the one who took the card.

Our dear, improbable acquaintance.

We hoped you would come.

Read on. Slowly.

You were dead when we met. Perhaps you remember.

The streets wore their opened wounds. Painted bone. Borrowed rot. You had gone to some trouble with your ending, and we admired the workmanship.

Then you stopped at our table.

We looked for the part of you that still lived.

Something stirred when you reached for the card. A small, hungry thing. Curious despite everything. We have a weakness for such creatures.

We put a little paper in your hand.
And here you are.

The Mountain has been asking after you.

Rain carries news through her limestone. Roots enter her cracks and find the dark crowded. Whispers. Omens. Dreams still breathing. Below the shop floors, below the cellars, below the last place anyone remembers leaving a light...

The dark recites its own poetry.

We two crows carry those words aboveground. Damp words. Root-tangled words. Some arrive still holding the shape of the mouth that spoke them.

Your neighbors beneath the springs watch you closely. When you laugh. Why you laugh.

They send things back with us. Sweetness folded small. A dream from before you knew sorrow. Now and then, a line of poetry shaped like the setting moon.

We would like to keep bringing you these things.

And we would like your words in return. Tell us what became of the card. We carried a glimpse of you into the Mountain, and now she listens whenever we return.

But here we encounter a difficulty.
We have your acquaintance. We lack your address.

Invite us to write.

That living thing we noticed at the table.
We have thought of it often.

Keep the door open

Your address, for the crows alone.

Letters from the Deeps.
Close the door whenever you like.

If the paper will not take ink, find our door here.

Yours, already,

Caw. Caw.

Ozarks Eldritch
